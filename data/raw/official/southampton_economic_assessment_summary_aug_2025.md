Southampton Economic Assessment – Summary of Findings, August 2025
Source: Southampton Data Observatory, Data, Intelligence & Insight Team (Southampton City Council), https://data.southampton.gov.uk/media/iqgh14rf/economic-assessment-august-2025.pdf
Text of the slide deck, slide by slide. Figures are as published.

## Slide 4 – Background to the assessment
- Extended the JSNA model to economic development: ensure decisions and strategic intent are based on the best available evidence; use appropriate analytical methods and statistics to turn data into intelligence.
- Benchmarking against ONS Statistical Neighbours; trend analysis of pre and post recession periods; deep dive analysis of demographics, industry and geography.
- ONS is the primary data source; time lag limitations mean some datasets represent the period before Covid and the Ukraine war. The team attempted to analyse the impact of COVID-19 and economic instability caused by the Ukraine war on the Southampton economy wherever possible.
- Baseline assessment based on available data.

## Slide 5 – Economic Geography
- The Good Growth report in 2024 highlights that the ongoing squeeze on living standards sees the UK public focus primarily on economic or finance focused measures, with people placing more importance on income, jobs, health and housing.
- The UK economy is recovering slowly and steadily, and economic growth is expected to continue to bounce back and grow.
- Southampton ranks 3rd (out of 51) in the latest Good Growth Cities Index, having previously ranked 5th in 2023.
- Southampton has an above average overall rating (0.59) and above average ratings for jobs (0.73), income (0.65), health (1.25), skills (1.63), environment (0.47) and safety (0.60). New businesses scored -0.05.
- Work-life balance (0.65), transport (0.38), income distribution (0.78) and high streets and shops (-0.44) have an average rating.
- Housing (-0.44) is the only indicator with a below average rating.
- Most reports on the local economy focus on the Hampshire Economic Area or the Southampton Travel to Work Area (TTWA); they are not specific to Southampton, so there is a need for intelligence on the Southampton (local authority boundary) economy.
- Due to methodology changes, comparison with PwC Good Growth rankings prior to 2022 is not possible.
- Source: PwC Good Growth for Cities report 2024.

## Slide 6 – Devolution
- In February 2025 the Government announced that Southampton City Council, Portsmouth City Council, Hampshire County Council and Isle of Wight Council had been successful in their joint bid for inclusion on the Government's fast-track Devolution Priority Programme (DPP).
- The proposed Combined County Authority would be known as the Hampshire and the Solent Combined County Authority.
- Devolution is the transfer of powers and funding from national to local government, so more decisions are made locally, closer to residents and businesses.
- The Mayoral Combined County Authority would receive devolved funding including for housing and regeneration, local growth, adult skills (except apprenticeships) and local transport.
- Source: Hampshire and the Solent devolution consultation.

## Slide 8 – Productivity and Growth: GVA revisions
- The ONS publishes GVA estimates annually, with the latest year provisional; previous years are subject to revisions and rebalancing.
- In the latest release (up to 2023) the ONS identified a change in the UK National Accounts that resulted in a long-term revision to the manufacturing of food, beverages and tobacco industry, concentrated in Southampton.
- The value of the manufacturing sector in 2021 was revised from £265 million to £2.1 billion, making manufacturing the most prominent industry in Southampton (16.3% share of total GVA in 2023).
- Southampton is not a traditional manufacturing city, but multi-national businesses in the city with economic ownership of overseas manufacturing operations are counted as manufacturing in GVA estimates.
- The ONS latest estimates suggest the Southampton economy was worth £10 billion in 2023.
- Revisions affected long-term trends, GVA per head and GVA by industry, so comparison with previously published estimates is not advised. The revisions are a real economic effect, the result of ONS measurements catching up with reality.

## Slide 9 – Productivity and Growth: GVA (B)
- Southampton's economy in 2023: £10 billion. Southampton represents 2.8% of the South East economy.
- The economy declined by -4.3% between 2019 and 2020, followed by increases of +5.4% in 2021, +12.2% in 2022 and +10.6% in 2023.
- Since 2019 this is an increase of +£1.1 billion (+12.9%). Change since 2019: England +23.2%, South East +19.7%, Southampton +12.9%.
- The England (+19.9%) and South East (+16.7%) economies also grew in the last two years (2021-23).
- This highlights positive economic recovery and growth since the COVID-19 pandemic. GVA rounded to nearest hundred million. Source: ONS GVA.

## Slide 10 – GVA (B) per head
- GVA (B) per head in Southampton (£38,980 in 2023) is higher than England (£36,632) and the South East (£37,385).
- Southampton's GVA per head increased +9.9% since 2019, compared with England +20.0% and the South East +16.0%.
- Southampton has the fourth highest GVA per head amongst ONS comparators (behind Leeds £44,072, Bristol £43,533 and York £41,162), highlighting relatively high productivity.

## Slide 11 – GVA per hour worked
- GVA per hour worked in Southampton in 2022 was £47.50, higher than the UK value of £39.70.
- Southampton saw a +1.1% increase in GVA per hour worked since 2021, the UK +1.5%.
- Southampton has the highest GVA per hour worked amongst ONS comparators (Portsmouth £41.50 next).

## Slide 12 – GVA (B) by industry, 2023 shares
- 16% of Southampton's GVA comes from manufacturing, compared to 9% in England. Manufacturing has been the most prominent industry since 2020 (25%), likely influenced by economic ownership of overseas manufacturing operations.
- Transportation and storage accounts for 12% of GVA locally compared to 3% nationally.
- 'Public sector' industries (public administration and defence, education and health) account for 27% of the Southampton economy, compared to 19% nationally (public administration 4%, education 9%, health and social work 14%).
- Southampton has a notably lower share of information and communication (2% vs 6%), real estate (8% vs 13%) and professional, scientific and technical activities (4% vs 9%). Wholesale and retail trade is 9% (England 10%); accommodation and food services 2% (England 3%).

## Slide 13 – Change in GVA by industry, 2021 to 2023
- Transportation and storage recovered strongly between 2021 and 2023 (+£346 million) but remains -£492 million lower than in 2019.
- Human health and social work saw the second highest increase (+£327 million). Financial and insurance +£230 million, education +£215 million, real estate +£120 million, wholesale and retail +£91 million, professional services +£71 million, other services +£66 million, public administration +£65 million, construction +£64 million, administrative and support +£61 million, accommodation and food +£36 million, arts and recreation +£31 million.
- 13 of the 18 industries analysed increased GVA between 2021 and 2023; 3 industries remain below 2019.
- Manufacturing increased +£113 million between 2019 and 2023 but saw the largest decline between 2021 and 2023 (-£453 million). Information and communication -£10 million.

## Slide 14 – GVA by industry, 1998 to 2023
- Manufacturing grew five-fold from 5% of total GVA in 2015 to a peak of 25% in 2020.
- Transportation and storage grew from 7% of GVA in 1998 to 19% in 2019, then fell to 10% in 2021 after the pandemic.
- Human health and social work rose gradually from 7% in 2000 to 14% in 2023.
- Financial and insurance activities fell from 18% of GVA in 2014 to 5% in 2020, then grew to 9% in 2023.

## Slide 16 – Business Growth and Density
- Southampton had a -2.8% (-215) decrease in the number of business enterprises over the last year (2023 to 2024), compared to -0.1% in the South East and -0.1% in England. Enterprises: 8,785 in 2021, 7,890 in 2022, 7,615 in 2023, 7,400 in 2024.
- Almost all the decline was micro enterprises (fewer than 10 employees, -185). Retail saw the largest decline by industry (-155), followed by construction (-35) and professional, scientific and technical (-30).
- Southampton has 421 business enterprises per 10,000 working age population, significantly lower than England (653) and the South East (692).
- Multiple business registrations at a single postcode may have affected data in 2019 and 2020: 670 (37%) of the 1,820 births in 2020 were at postcodes with multiple registrations.
- Source: ONS UK business: activity, size and location.

## Slide 17 – Enterprises by industry
- Retail accounted for 14% of enterprises in Southampton in 2024, compared to 8% nationally. Construction 15% (England 14%). Accommodation and food 8% (England 6%).
- Transport and storage has 10% of enterprises, over double the national share (4%); professional, scientific and technical is smaller (12% vs 16%).
- Change 2023 to 2024: retail -155, construction -35, professional -30, wholesale -20, transport -20, finance -10, education -10, production -5; increases were limited: accommodation and food +25, health +15, property +10, motor trades +10, business administration +5, agriculture +5.

## Slide 18 – Business start-ups and deaths
- There were 1,005 business start-ups (births) in Southampton in 2023, a decline of -29.5% (-420) on the previous year. The birth rate is significantly lower than the national average.
- There were 1,175 business deaths in 2023, a decrease of -17.0% (-240). Business death rate 66.8 per 10k working age population.
- 2023 was the first decrease in enterprise deaths since 2019. Previous increases in death rates are likely affected by multiple registrations.

## Slide 19 – Births, deaths and net growth rates, 2023
- Birth rate 57.1 businesses per 10k working age population (South East 73.5, England 77.7).
- Death rate 66.8 per 10k (South East 72.4, England 76.0).
- Net growth rate -9.7 per 10k working age population (second lowest among comparators after Coventry -10.7).

## Slide 20 – Business survival in the first year
- 1-year survival in Southampton decreased from 93.4% in 2022 to 92.3% in 2023, similar to the national average (92.4%). Survival may have been affected by multiple registrations in 2020.

## Slide 21 – Business survival, first 2 years
- 68.4% of businesses formed in Southampton in 2021 survived at least 2 years (England 70.3%, South East 73.6%). Earlier 2-year survival is likely skewed by multiple registrations.

## Slide 22 – Business survival, longer term
- 3-year survival in Southampton is 35.2%, lower than the national average (52.8%) and down from 50.8% the previous year; affected by multiple registrations in 2020.
- 5-year survival is 33.1%, lower than England (39.4%).
- The pandemic and recent economic uncertainty could continue to create obstacles for longer-term business survival.

## Slide 23 – Business floorspace (Valuation Office Agency)
- In 2022/23 there was 1,741 thousand m2 of business floorspace in Southampton (published as "1,741 m2").
- Since 2000/01 total floorspace fell -17.2%, compared to increases of 1.7% in England and 4.6% in the South East.
- Office (-40.2%) and industrial (-24.6%) floorspace reduced most.
- Industrial is still the largest class (39.5%) but much lower than England (58.0%), second lowest among comparators. Office 14.5%, other 15.7%.
- 30.2% of floorspace is retail, the largest share amongst comparator cities.

## Slide 25 – Job postings (Lightcast, March 2025)
- In 2024 Southampton had 344 unique job postings per 1,000 working age population, significantly higher than England (208) and the South East (226). Rates: 2022 412.6, 2023 435.0, 2024 343.6.
- Postings decreased for Southampton and all comparators in 2024. Southampton ranks second highest amongst ONS comparators (after Bristol, 541).
- Median advertised wages averaged £31.8k per year in 2024, above the long-term average.
- Southampton remains a major employment centre, but it is important to attract higher value businesses and more skilled jobs that residents can benefit from.

## Slide 26 – Employee job density
- Ratio of jobs to working age population is 0.66, the lowest among comparators (England 0.76).
- Job density is 23.1 jobs per hectare, fourth highest among comparators.

## Slide 27 – Employee jobs (BRES)
- 115,269 employee jobs in Southampton in 2023, an increase of 2.5% (2,845) on 2022 (South East and England +1.3%).
- Jobs are similar to 2015 (net +10), while England grew +9.8% and the South East +6.2%.
- Private sector employees +2,536 (+3.0%): full-time +1,380 (+2.4%), part-time +1,156 (+4.0%). Public sector +309 (+1.1%): full-time +293 (+1.6%), part-time +16 (+0.2%).

## Slide 28 – Employee jobs by industry, 2023
- Health, education, retail and business administration and support are the largest employers, together over half of jobs.
- Health 27,000; education 13,000; retail 10,000; business administration 9,000; transport and storage 9,000; professional services 8,000; accommodation and food 8,000; public administration 6,000; arts and entertainment 4,500; financial 4,000; manufacturing 4,000; information and communication 3,000; construction 3,000; wholesale 2,500; motor trades 1,750; property 1,250.
- Public sector industries provide 40% of jobs locally (27% nationally). Health is 24% of jobs (England 14%). Retail 9% (England 8%); accommodation and food 7% (England 8%).

## Slide 29 – Location quotients
- Health (27,000 jobs) is the largest employer with a location quotient above 1.
- Education (13,000), retail (10,000), transport and storage (9,000), public administration and defence (6,000) and financial and insurance (4,000) also have a higher share locally than nationally.

## Slide 30 – Change in employee jobs by industry, 2022 to 2023
- Job losses: business administration and support (-1,000), manufacturing (-500), construction (-500), motor trades (-500), information and communication (-500), agriculture (-25).
- Jobs created in five industries: health (+2,000), education (+1,000), financial and insurance (+1,000), property (+250), wholesale (+250). Retail, accommodation and food, transport and professional services were unchanged.

## Slide 31 – Self-employment
- 7.2% (9,500) of people in employment in Southampton are self-employed, significantly lower than England (12.3%) and the South East (12.1%); a non-significant decrease from 12.2% in 2022/23.

## Slide 33 – Economic activity
- In 2023/24, 79.2% (137,300) of working age residents were economically active (England 78.8%, South East 82.3%); down -0.9 percentage points (-2,000) in the last year.
- Main reasons for inactivity: student 22.7% (England 27.0%), looking after family/home 30.5%, long-term sick 21.9%.

## Slide 34 – Employment
- Employment rate 76.0% (131,900) in 2023/24 (England 75.7%, South East 79.6%). Lowest point 68.1% in 2009/10. Recent changes not statistically significant.

## Slide 35 – Unemployment
- 5,400 people unemployed, 4.0% of the working age population (2023/24), down from 4.3% (2022/23), 600 fewer. Changes not statistically significant.

## Slide 36 – Claimant count
- 4.5% (7,910) of working age residents were claiming out of work benefits in February 2025, up +640 (+8.8%) from February 2024 (4.1%). England +12.6%, South East +10.6%.
- Southampton is yet to return to the pre-pandemic baseline (below 3.5% in January to March 2020).

## Slide 37 – Claimant count inequalities (June 2025)
- Higher claimant counts are in neighbourhoods in Thornhill, Woolston, Bevois and Redbridge wards, where some of the most deprived neighbourhoods are.
- The gap between the most and least deprived neighbourhoods fell from a peak of 8.1 percentage points in April 2021 to 4.9 in June 2025.

## Slide 39 – Skills: degrees
- 52.1% (69,100) of the economically active population were qualified to RQF4+ (degree level) in 2024 (England 53.0%, South East 53.4%). It fell from 53.6% in 2022 to 44.4% in 2023, then rose again; changes not statistically significant.

## Slide 40 – Skills: A-levels or equivalent
- 23.5% (31,100) qualified to RQF3 in 2024, higher but not significantly than England (20.2%) and the South East (21.1%), likely due to the large student population.

## Slide 41 – Skills: no qualifications
- 4.0% (5,300) have no qualifications or RQF1 only, down from 7.7% in 2023. No qualifications fell from 3.7% to 1.5%; RQF1 only from 4.0% to 2.5%.

## Slide 42 – School attainment (2023/24)
- Average Attainment 8 score 41.3, third lowest among comparators (England 46.1).
- 38.3% of pupils achieved grade 5 or above in English and Maths, third lowest, below the national 46.2%.

## Slide 43 – Higher education
- 35,645 higher education students in Southampton in 2023/24; 73% (25,995) at the University of Southampton; Solent University 9,650.
- +0.9% (+320) on the previous year, below the 2011/12 peak (36,665). Postgraduates -6.5% (-735); undergraduates +4.5% (+1,070).
- The universities are valuable assets for employment, workforce skills and knowledge-based industries; the city should aim to improve graduate retention.

## Slide 44 – Residents who attended Southampton universities
- 12.8% of residents with online job profiles attended the University of Southampton (6.8%) or Solent University (6.0%); mid-rank among comparators. 18,874 profiles considered.

## Slides 45–46 – Graduate retention (Travel To Work Area, 2018 to 2019)
- Southampton TTWA graduate share 32.4% (England median 25.3%); participation rate 32.6% (29.7%); retention rate 57.0% (51.3%); inward migration rate 14.0% (8.0%); net gain rate -0.1% (-20.1%).

## Slide 48 – Earnings benchmarking (ASHE 2024)
- Weekly pay for full-time workers who work in Southampton was £716 in 2024 (£704 in 2023). Residents earn £690, lower than England (£732) and the South East (£779).
- Resident wages rose 4.7% (£31) vs South East 7.0% and England 6.1%; workplace wages rose 0.4% (£3) vs South East 6.3% and England 6.1%.

## Slide 49 – Inflation
- CPI inflation rose from 2.0% in July 2021 to a peak of 11.1% in October 2022, then fell to 2.8% in February 2025, still above the 2% target. The OBR forecasts a return to target in 2029. Lower income households are hit hardest because they spend more of their income on necessities.

## Slides 50–51 – Earnings trends and inflation
- Adjusted for inflation, resident earnings rose (+£15, +2.3%) between 2023 and 2024 while workplace earnings fell (-£14, -1.9%).
- Between 2021 and 2024, adjusted resident earnings fell -£47 (-6.4%) and workplace earnings -£65 (-8.3%). PwC estimates real earnings are lower than in 2006.

## Slide 52 – Workplace vs resident earnings
- Workplace earnings are £26 (3.8%) a week more than resident earnings (2024), the fourth largest gap among comparators. High workplace earnings suggest skilled jobs exist in the city, but commuters into the city hold many of them.

## Slide 53 – Male vs female pay
- Full-time resident gender pay gap 16.4% (£120) in 2024 (England £112, 14.3%); workplace gap 17.6% (£141). No evidence the gap is narrowing.

## Slides 54–55 – Economic flows (Census 2021)
- Over 24,000 people commute into Southampton for work and almost 29,500 commute out; net in-commute -5,433, second lowest among comparators.
- 75.1% of working residents live and work in the area, the lowest among comparator cities.
- Top flows are Eastleigh, New Forest and Test Valley (residents commuting to: Eastleigh 30.5%, New Forest 14.8%, Test Valley 14.7%, Winchester 12.2%; workers commuting from: Eastleigh 28.6%, New Forest 20.6%, Test Valley 13.2%).
- Home working rose from 10.3% in 2011 to 31.2% in 2021.

## Slides 57–58 – Summary of findings
- The Southampton economy is worth £10 billion, +£1.1 billion (+12.9%) on 2019.
- An ONS revision to food, beverages and tobacco manufacturing, concentrated in Southampton, makes manufacturing the most prominent industry.
- 13 of 18 industries grew GVA; 3 remain below 2019, notably transport and storage (-£492 million). Manufacturing fell most between 2021 and 2023 (-£453 million).
- Businesses fell -2.8% between 2023 and 2024, driven by micro enterprises; retail, construction and professional services declined most.
- Construction has the largest share of enterprises (15%), then retail (14%, vs 8% nationally).
- Employee jobs rose +2.5% between 2022 and 2023.
- Southampton remains a major employment centre but should attract higher value businesses and skilled jobs; the workplace–resident earnings gap is £26 a week.
- Unemployment fell from 4.3% to 4.0%. Residents qualified to RQF4+ rose from 44.4% in 2023 to 53.8% in 2024 (as stated in the summary). KS4 pupil performance ranks low.
- Residents are possibly being displaced into lower paid work outside the city by skilled in-commuters.
